Trump Designates Oval Office and Cabinet Room as a National Monument

Published November 6, 2026 · 2:33 p.m. ET · Rajasthan News 24 US Desk

President Donald Trump signed a Nov. 5 proclamation designating the Oval Office, Outer Oval Office and Cabinet Room as the Oval Office National Monument, with preservation review for substantial alterations.

WASHINGTON, November 6, 2026: President Donald Trump signed a Nov. 5 proclamation designating the Oval Office, Outer Oval Office and Cabinet Room as the Oval Office National Monument, with preservation review for substantial alterations.

What the designation covers

The proclamation defines the monument as the Oval Office (Room 110), the Outer Oval Office (Rooms 106 and 108) and the Cabinet Room (Room 105). It invokes the Antiquities Act and directs the Interior secretary, through the National Park Service, to develop a management plan with White House officials and other agencies.

The plan is meant to prioritize the rooms' structural and significant architectural features. Before an action that would substantially alter or demolish the monument or its essential elements, officials must document the proposal, review it and consider alternatives.

What the order does not prohibit

The proclamation expressly says it does not restrict the president's authority to redecorate, select furnishings or make ordinary operational and security changes, provided they do not substantially impair the historic structure or architectural character. It also says the action does not alter existing White House exemptions under the National Historic Preservation Act or diminish Congress's authority over federal property.

Why it matters

The designation creates a formal preservation framework for rooms that have served as the center of presidential work for generations. The White House describes the move as a way to protect them from substantial alteration. The Associated Press notes that a future president could reverse the designation; it is not a guarantee that the rooms can never change.

The practical distinction is between major structural changes, which trigger documentation and review, and ordinary changes that the proclamation leaves within presidential authority.
