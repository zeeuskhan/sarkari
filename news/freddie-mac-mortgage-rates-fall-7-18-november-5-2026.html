Average 30-Year Mortgage Rate Falls to 7.18%, Its Third Straight Weekly Decline

Published November 6, 2026 · 2:04 p.m. ET · Rajasthan News 24 US Desk

The average U.S. 30-year fixed mortgage rate fell to 7.18% for the week ending November 5, according to Freddie Mac. The 15-year rate also edged lower, offering modest relief while borrowing costs remain high.

MCLEAN, VIRGINIA, November 6, 2026: The average U.S. 30-year fixed mortgage rate fell to 7.18% for the week ending November 5, according to Freddie Mac. The 15-year rate also edged lower, offering modest relief while borrowing costs remain high.

The average rate on a 30-year fixed mortgage fell to 7.18% for the week ending November 5, down from 7.24% a week earlier, according to Freddie Mac’s Primary Mortgage Market Survey. It was the third consecutive weekly decline. The average 15-year fixed rate slipped to 6.57% from 6.60%.

What the survey shows

Freddie Mac’s archive lists the 30-year rate at 7.32% on October 22 and 7.40% on October 15. The latest reading marks a gradual retreat from mid-October, although the average remains above 7%. These figures are national weekly averages for conventional, conforming home-purchase loans, not quotes available to every borrower.

A lender’s offer can differ based on credit score, down payment, loan size, points, property type, loan program and the date the rate is locked.

Why mortgage rates move

The Federal Reserve does not set 30-year mortgage rates directly. Long-term Treasury yields, mortgage-backed securities, lender funding costs and investor demand influence the rates lenders offer. Inflation expectations and Fed policy matter, but a change in the federal funds rate does not translate mechanically into the same change in mortgage pricing.

What a lower average means for buyers

A small weekly decline can improve purchasing power at the margin, but it does not solve broader affordability challenges. Buyers still have to account for home prices, property taxes, insurance, closing costs and maintenance. Home shoppers should compare written loan estimates from multiple lenders and consider the APR, fees, points and total interest over the expected time in the home.
