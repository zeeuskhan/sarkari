Federal College Child-Care Program Awards 40 Grants, Far Below 148 Expected

Published November 6, 2026 · 2:04 p.m. ET · Rajasthan News 24 US Desk

WASHINGTON, November 6, 2026: The federal Child Care Access Means Parents in School program awarded 40 four-year grants, well below the 148 awards the Education Department had expected. Colleges say the shortfall is disrupting child-care support for low-income student parents.

The federal Child Care Access Means Parents in School program, known as CCAMPIS, awarded 40 four-year grants in its latest competition, far fewer than the 148 awards the Education Department had anticipated, according to reporting by The Washington Post. The program helps colleges provide campus-based or community child care for low-income student parents.

What the program supports

The Education Department describes CCAMPIS as a competitive grant program for institutions of higher education. Schools can use the money to establish or support child-care programs that primarily serve low-income students, including sliding-scale fees and other assistance that helps parents remain enrolled.

The Department announced the 2026 competition in April, saying more than $73.5 million would be available. Its program page lists 327 awards in fiscal 2021 and 264 in fiscal 2023, showing how much broader earlier award rounds were.

Why fewer awards matter

Campus child care can be the difference between attending a lab, clinical placement or evening class and reducing a course load. Colleges that expected grant support may have to find replacement funding, reduce subsidies or change the number of families they can serve. Students can face higher out-of-pocket costs or longer delays in completing a degree.

The Washington Post reported that the department had expected to fund 148 institutions but awarded 40 four-year grants. Officials said the department was concentrating funds on successful applicants amid uncertainty about future budgets.

What students and colleges should check

Institutions should review official award notices and contact the Department of Education about future rounds. Student parents should ask campus child-care offices about local subsidies, sliding-scale fees, emergency grants, state assistance and community providers. CCAMPIS grants are awarded to eligible colleges, not paid directly to individual students.
