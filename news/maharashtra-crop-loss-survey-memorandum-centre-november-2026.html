Maharashtra to Seek Central Assistance After Large-Scale Crop-Loss Assessment

Maharashtra has conducted nearly three crore field-site visits to assess crop damage linked to low rainfall and is preparing a memorandum seeking assistance from the Centre.

Published November 1, 2026 · Updated November 2, 2026 · Rajasthan News 24 Desk

Mumbai, November 1, 2026: Maharashtra is preparing to seek additional assistance from the Union government after assessing crop damage linked to low rainfall. The state's agriculture department has reportedly carried out nearly three crore field-site visits to collect information on affected crops.

Media reports said the scale of this year's assessment is much larger than an earlier exercise. Officials are compiling and checking the records before the state submits a memorandum to the Centre. The state government was reported to be preparing the document for submission within the next few days.

How is crop damage being recorded?

Reports describe the use of geo-tagged photographs and field-level records to document affected land. Such records can help identify the location and condition of a field, but they still need to be checked and consolidated before they can support a formal relief request.

What does this mean for farmers?

Submitting a memorandum is a request for assistance, not confirmation that additional funds have been approved. The amount, eligibility rules and payment schedule will depend on the assessment and subsequent government decisions.

Farmers should check notices from their local tehsil office and the state's official relief and rehabilitation department for information about surveys, eligibility and any required documentation. They should not assume that a payment date has been announced until an official order confirms it.

How crop-loss assessments are generally carried out

After damaging rainfall, drought, hail or other extreme weather, state officials may conduct field inspections and compile crop-loss estimates. The assessment can consider the affected area, crop type, stage of growth, extent of damage and the number of farmers or villages affected. Revenue and agriculture officials may use field reports, local records and other available evidence. The final estimate can change as additional areas are inspected and data is reconciled.

Why a memorandum to the Centre matters

A state memorandum is a formal request for assistance, not an automatic sanction of funds. It generally sets out the scale of damage, the districts involved, the estimated financial impact and the relief requested. The Union government may examine the submission under the applicable disaster-response framework and seek additional information or an assessment before deciding what assistance is permissible. The amount sought by a state and the amount ultimately approved can therefore differ.

What support may be available to affected farmers

Depending on the nature of the event and applicable rules, support can involve relief from state disaster-response funds, central assistance, crop-insurance claims, input subsidies or other state-specific measures. Each channel has different eligibility conditions and documentation. A farmer should not assume that a survey entry guarantees a fixed payment. The official district administration, agriculture department and insurance provider are the appropriate sources for individual claim status.

Steps farmers can take after crop damage

  • Report the damage to the local revenue or agriculture office within the announced window.
  • Keep land-record details, crop information and bank details available for verification.
  • For insured crops, notify the insurer or designated claims channel within the policy deadline.
  • Save acknowledgement numbers, photographs and copies of any application.
  • Follow district notices for survey visits, objections and relief disbursement lists.

Why insurance deadlines are especially important

Crop-insurance schemes can impose strict timelines for reporting prevented sowing, localised calamities or post-harvest losses. The applicable deadline depends on the policy and type of damage. Farmers should use the official scheme portal, insurer helpline or agriculture office to confirm the correct process. Waiting for a general relief announcement may cause a separate insurance notification deadline to be missed.

How to assess claims about the size of losses

Early estimates are often provisional. A meaningful assessment should state the period, districts, crops, hectares or acres covered, the method used and whether the figure refers to total affected area or severely damaged area. Readers should distinguish the state's estimate from an independent assessment and from the final amount approved for assistance. These figures answer different questions and should not be presented as if they were identical.

What happens next

The next steps are publication of district-level assessment details, submission of the memorandum, any central review and communication of approved relief measures. Farmers should follow official notices rather than relying on forwarded lists or agents promising guaranteed payments.
