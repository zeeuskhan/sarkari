U.S. Consumer Sentiment Improves in November, but High Prices Still Dominate Concerns

Published November 6, 2026 · 2:04 p.m. ET · Rajasthan News 24 US Desk

The University of Michigan’s preliminary consumer-sentiment index rose to 51.0 in November from 48.0 in October. The improvement was modest against persistent concern about prices and recent market volatility.

ANN ARBOR, November 6, 2026: The University of Michigan’s preliminary consumer-sentiment index rose to 51.0 in November from 48.0 in October. The improvement was modest against persistent concern about prices and recent market volatility.

U.S. consumer sentiment improved in the preliminary November reading from the University of Michigan, rising to 51.0 from 48.0 in October. The index was unchanged from November 2025, showing that the monthly improvement has not restored confidence over the past year.

What improved?

The current economic conditions index rose to 51.0 from 46.7 in October. The expectations index increased to 50.9 from 48.8. The university said modest gains were concentrated mainly among lower- and middle-wealth households.

Sentiment fell 5% among respondents with the largest stock portfolios after a turbulent period in financial markets. Overall sentiment remained about 10% below its February level.

Inflation expectations eased, but price pressure remains

Year-ahead inflation expectations declined to 4.3% from 4.7% in October. Long-run expectations edged down to 3.4% from 3.5%. About 57% of consumers spontaneously mentioned that high prices had eroded their living standards, the highest share since May and up from 46% in February.

What the survey can and cannot tell us

The preliminary report is based on interviews conducted from October 20 through November 2. It measures how people feel about their finances and the economy; it is not a direct measure of retail sales, household income or the inflation rate. Actual spending can differ from survey responses, and a preliminary reading can change when the final sample is included.

The University of Michigan scheduled the final November results for November 20 at 10 a.m. Eastern time. Policymakers and investors will compare the final reading with inflation, employment and spending data before drawing conclusions about the consumer outlook.
