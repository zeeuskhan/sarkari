PM-KISAN 24th Installment 2026: How Farmers Can Check Payment Status and Complete eKYC

The government says the 24th PM-KISAN instalment is scheduled for 30 October 2026. Learn how to check status, complete eKYC and understand verification holds.

Published November 2, 2026 · Rajasthan News 24 Desk

New Delhi, November 2, 2026: The official PM-KISAN portal says the 24th instalment of the Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi scheme is scheduled for release on October 30, 2026, in Banaskantha, Gujarat. Farmers should use the official portal or mobile application to check their individual payment status rather than relying on forwarded messages or unofficial websites.

What is PM-KISAN?

PM-KISAN is a central government income-support scheme for eligible landholding farmer families. The National Portal of India describes the benefit as ₹6,000 per year, paid in three equal instalments of ₹2,000, subject to scheme rules and exclusions. A scheduled national release does not mean every registered farmer will necessarily receive a payment on the same day: eligibility, bank validation and verification can affect individual records.

How to check your PM-KISAN status

  1. Open the official PM-KISAN website.
  2. Find the farmer services or “Know Your Status” facility.
  3. Enter the requested registration or identity details on the official page.
  4. Review the displayed status, including eligibility, eKYC and payment information.
  5. If the portal shows a discrepancy, contact the designated agriculture office or an authorised Common Service Centre (CSC).

Why eKYC matters

The PM-KISAN homepage states that eKYC is mandatory for registered farmers. It also says biometric-based eKYC is available through CSC VLE logins, the PM-KISAN mobile application and authorised state-user logins. Farmers should not share OTPs, Aadhaar details or bank credentials with callers claiming they can release an instalment.

Why a payment may be held

The official portal currently flags suspected cases that may fall under scheme exclusion rules. Examples listed include land ownership acquired after February 1, 2019, and cases where more than one family member appears to be receiving benefits. The portal says benefits in suspected cases may be temporarily withheld while physical verification is completed. Farmers should check their own “Know Your Status” record and follow the official verification process rather than assuming that a delay automatically means permanent rejection.

Important precautions

  • Use only the government PM-KISAN portal or authorised service channels.
  • Check that the bank account and Aadhaar-related details in the official record are correct.
  • Do not pay an agent who guarantees approval or immediate release.
  • Keep application references and acknowledgements for follow-up.

Bottom line

The 24th instalment date is listed on the official portal, but each farmer's entitlement depends on eligibility and successful verification. The safest next step is to check the official status page and resolve any eKYC or record issue through authorised channels.

Why a scheduled release may not appear in every bank account

A national instalment announcement is not the same as confirmation that every beneficiary has passed all checks. Payment can depend on whether the farmer's record remains eligible, whether eKYC is complete, whether land details have been verified and whether the bank account is correctly linked and active. A payment may also be delayed by a name mismatch, inactive account, incorrect IFSC information or a pending verification at the state or district level.

How to understand the status displayed on the portal

Read each field separately. A record may show that registration exists while eKYC or land-seeding verification is still pending. A payment status may refer to an earlier instalment rather than the latest release. If a transaction is marked as processed, check the bank statement and the date shown in the official portal. If it is rejected or returned, note the reason code and ask the bank or designated agriculture office how it can be corrected.

What to do if eKYC is incomplete

Use the official PM-KISAN website or the official mobile application to check the available eKYC methods. Depending on the service and current rules, authentication may be available through Aadhaar-based OTP or biometric assistance at an authorised Common Service Centre. Make sure the mobile number used for OTP verification is accessible. Never give an OTP to a caller or agent who contacts you independently.

Common reasons for payment holds

  • eKYC is incomplete or identity details do not match.
  • Land records have not been verified or are inconsistent.
  • The bank account is closed, inactive or has incorrect details.
  • Aadhaar-bank linking or payment mapping requires correction.
  • The farmer's record needs eligibility review under scheme exclusions.

How farmers in Rajasthan can seek help

Farmers should first review the status on the official PM-KISAN portal. If the issue relates to land records, contact the local revenue or agriculture office with the relevant account details. If the issue is bank-related, speak directly with the bank and ask whether the account can receive the applicable direct-benefit transfer. An authorised CSC may help with online services, but the farmer should receive an acknowledgement and understand any displayed service charge.

Beware of fake instalment messages

Scammers may circulate links claiming that a farmer must pay a small fee, update a bank card or share an OTP to receive an instalment. Do not click unknown links or install remote-access applications at the request of a caller. The government does not need a farmer's UPI PIN or ATM PIN to credit a benefit. Check the domain carefully and use only official government portals.

Keep records for future instalments

Save the registration number, UAN or scheme reference where applicable, eKYC confirmation and bank acknowledgement. Check that the name and account details are consistent across records. If a correction is submitted, retain its reference number and check again after the relevant department has processed it. Accurate records reduce the chance of repeated holds and make it easier to explain a problem to the responsible office.
