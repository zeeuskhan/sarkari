U.S. Money-Market Funds Take In $117.97 Billion in Biggest Weekly Inflow Since May

Published November 6, 2026 · 2:04 p.m. ET · Rajasthan News 24 US Desk

Investors added a net $117.97 billion to U.S. money-market funds in the week through November 4, the largest weekly inflow in six months. Equity and bond funds also recorded net inflows.

NEW YORK, November 6, 2026: Investors added a net $117.97 billion to U.S. money-market funds in the week through November 4, the largest weekly inflow in six months. Equity and bond funds also recorded net inflows.

U.S. money-market funds attracted $117.97 billion in net inflows during the week ended November 4, according to LSEG Lipper data cited by Reuters. It was the largest weekly net investment in the category since May 6. The move came amid caution around the midterm elections and concern about the cost and cash needs of major technology companies investing in AI.

Cash funds gained, but investors did not abandon stocks

U.S. equity funds still attracted $14.81 billion in net inflows, reversing the previous week’s $6.54 billion outflow. Large-cap funds received $10.35 billion, multi-cap funds took in $1.64 billion and small-cap funds added about $423 million. Mid-cap funds recorded a $1.07 billion outflow.

Bond funds drew another $7.31 billion, their strongest weekly inflow in four weeks. Longer-maturity government and Treasury funds were among the categories attracting money.

Which sectors saw withdrawals?

Technology funds recorded an outflow of about $160 million. Industrial funds lost roughly $625 million, while communication-services funds shed about $966 million. Financial funds attracted $1.3 billion and healthcare funds gained $1.09 billion. These are weekly fund-flow estimates, not forecasts of sector performance.

Why investors may be holding more cash

Investors were reassessing AI valuations, infrastructure spending and the possibility of tighter oversight. Higher uncertainty can make short-term cash-like investments more attractive while people wait for clearer signals. But a large weekly inflow is not proof that a market crash is coming. The simultaneous inflows into equity and bond funds show that investors were making different choices across asset classes.

Money-market mutual funds are investment products, not bank savings accounts. Their yields, expenses, liquidity rules and risks differ from bank deposits. Anyone considering a move should check the product type and read the fund’s prospectus. These aggregate figures are a market snapshot, not personal financial advice.
