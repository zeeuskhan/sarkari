U.S. Corporate Bond Sales Hit a Record $1.88 Trillion as Big Tech Borrows for AI

Published November 6, 2026 · 2:04 p.m. ET · Rajasthan News 24 US Desk

NEW YORK, November 6, 2026: U.S. investment-grade corporate bond issuance reached $1.88 trillion through November 4, exceeding the previous annual record set in 2020. Major technology companies accounted for a substantial share as they financed AI infrastructure.

U.S. investment-grade corporate bond issuance reached $1.88 trillion through November 4, according to LSEG data reported by Reuters. That surpassed the previous annual record of $1.86 trillion, set in 2020. The 2026 figure is a year-to-date total, not a final full-year number.

Big Tech accounts for a large share

Amazon and Alphabet were among the largest issuers. Together with Meta, Microsoft and Oracle, major cloud and technology companies raised more than $370.7 billion through bond sales, roughly one-fifth of U.S. investment-grade issuance so far this year.

The number of deals was about 4% lower than a year earlier, at 1,084. The average offering size rose to roughly $1.7 billion from about $1.4 billion. That suggests the record was driven partly by larger transactions from the biggest borrowers rather than a surge in the number of issuers.

Apple was also preparing a seven-part U.S. dollar bond offering of about $7.5 billion, according to a term sheet reviewed by Reuters.

AI investment is changing corporate borrowing

Hyperscalers are spending heavily on data centers, chips, networking and power capacity. Bond markets have remained open to many of these borrowers, but higher rates increase debt-service costs. Delays in AI monetization could make it harder to earn returns quickly enough to justify large capital programs.

Reuters reported that credit-default insurance costs for some major AI spenders had risen, with Oracle’s five-year spread reaching a record 325.45 basis points in October.

What analysts expect

Goldman Sachs analysts estimated that bond issuance could fund about 35% of hyperscalers’ capital spending in 2027. That is an estimate, not a company commitment or a guaranteed financing mix. Actual borrowing will depend on investment plans, cash flow, interest rates and investor demand.

Record issuance alone does not mean a credit crisis is approaching. Investors should assess each issuer’s debt load, interest coverage, cash flow and maturity schedule, and whether new AI infrastructure can generate enough revenue to cover operating costs and interest expense.
