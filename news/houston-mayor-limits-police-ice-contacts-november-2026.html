Houston Mayor Signs Order to Limit Police Cooperation With ICE

Published November 6, 2026 · 2:04 p.m. ET · Rajasthan News 24 US Desk

Houston Mayor John Whitmire said the city would limit cooperation with federal immigration authorities, including avoiding ICE contact over nonviolent, non-jailable traffic offenses.

HOUSTON, November 6, 2026: Houston Mayor John Whitmire said the city would limit cooperation with federal immigration authorities, including avoiding ICE contact over nonviolent, non-jailable traffic offenses.

Houston Mayor John Whitmire said Friday that the city would limit cooperation with federal immigration authorities, describing a shift toward focusing enforcement on people who pose a danger rather than residents going about family, work and school activities. The announcement followed the midterm election results in Texas.

What the mayor said would change

Whitmire said a traffic incident involving a nonviolent, non-jailable offense would not by itself prompt contact with U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement. He described the approach as a way to reduce fear and anxiety for residents while keeping the city focused on public safety.

The Associated Press reported that the details were to be spelled out in an executive order. The mayor’s public description is not a substitute for the full written policy, including any exceptions and procedures for officers.

What the policy does not mean

The announcement concerns how Houston police interact with federal immigration authorities. It does not end federal immigration enforcement in the city or change ICE’s federal authority. The final order will determine how officers are instructed to handle different situations.

State and federal legal questions may shape how far a city can go in limiting cooperation. No conclusion about a court challenge should be drawn before the final order is reviewed and any legal action is filed.

Why it matters

Houston is one of the largest U.S. cities and has a substantial immigrant population. Local police departments balance cooperation with federal agencies against community trust, crime reporting and local resources. The practical effect will depend on training, enforcement and public understanding of the final rules.
