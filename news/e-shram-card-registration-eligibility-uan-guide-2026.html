e-Shram Card 2026: Eligibility, Registration, UAN and How Unorganised Workers Can Apply

Understand who can register on e-Shram, what the Universal Account Number means, how to use official registration channels and how to avoid fraud.

Published November 2, 2026 · Rajasthan News 24 Desk

New Delhi, November 2, 2026: India's e-Shram portal maintains a national database of unorganised workers, including construction workers, migrant workers, street vendors, domestic workers, agricultural workers and gig or platform workers. The system is intended to help improve identification and delivery of social-security services to workers who may not be covered by formal employment records.

Who can register?

The National Informatics Centre's e-Shram project page says unorganised-sector workers aged 16 to 59 are eligible to register. It lists groups such as construction workers, migrant workers, gig and platform workers, street vendors, domestic workers and agricultural workers. Eligibility and exclusions should always be checked on the official portal before registration.

What is the UAN?

After registration, a worker is assigned a Universal Account Number (UAN). The NIC description says the UAN card can be downloaded after registration. The UAN is an identifying number for the e-Shram database. It should not be mistaken for an automatic approval for every government benefit or a guarantee of a fixed monthly payment.

How to register through official channels

  1. Open the official e-Shram portal.
  2. Use the self-registration facility if you can complete the required online verification.
  3. If you need help, use an authorised Common Service Centre (CSC) or an applicable state service centre.
  4. Keep your mobile phone available for verification and provide information only on the official portal or to an authorised operator.
  5. After successful registration, save your UAN and download the card if the service makes it available.

What information may be requested?

The NIC project description lists Aadhaar KYC, mobile-number verification, bank-account status and details such as education, occupation and skills among the data handled in the registration workflow. Keep the information accurate. If a detail is wrong, use the official correction or assistance channel rather than creating duplicate registrations.

Does an e-Shram card guarantee financial assistance?

No. Registration creates a record in the national database. Access to a particular welfare scheme depends on that scheme's separate eligibility conditions, application process and current government rules. Be cautious of messages claiming that every e-Shram cardholder is entitled to a guaranteed monthly cash payment or that a fee is required to unlock a benefit.

Where can workers get help?

The NIC e-Shram page lists a toll-free helpdesk number, 14434, and says assistance is available in multiple languages from Monday to Saturday. Check the current contact information on the official e-Shram website before calling, as helpdesk arrangements can change.

Fraud-prevention checklist

  • Use the official eshram.gov.in domain or a recognised service centre.
  • Never share your bank PIN, card CVV or UPI PIN with an operator.
  • Do not pay someone who promises guaranteed benefits or priority approval.
  • Keep your UAN private and use official channels for corrections.

Conclusion

e-Shram is a national worker database designed to support access to social-security programmes. Workers should register through official channels, keep their details accurate and check each scheme's rules separately before expecting a benefit.

Who may not qualify and why details matter

The e-Shram database is intended for eligible unorganised workers, but not every person who is self-employed or temporarily without a job automatically qualifies. The official eligibility conditions and exclusions must be checked at the time of registration. A person covered by the relevant organised-sector social-security arrangements or otherwise excluded under the current rules should not submit an inaccurate declaration merely to obtain a card. If a worker has more than one occupation, they should describe their principal work accurately and follow the portal's instructions.

What happens after registration?

Registration creates a record and a UAN that can help identify a worker in the national database. It does not mean that a pension, insurance payment, cash transfer or job placement has been automatically approved. Separate welfare programmes may use e-Shram data as one part of their identification or delivery process, but each programme has its own eligibility rules, application steps and funding conditions. Workers should read the official scheme notification before assuming that registration alone qualifies them for a benefit.

How to correct a mistake

If a name, mobile number, occupation or other detail is incorrect, use the official portal's update facility where available or ask an authorised service centre for help. Keep the UAN and the acknowledgement from the original registration. Avoid creating multiple records to fix a spelling error. Duplicate registrations can make verification harder and may delay access to services. If a correction cannot be completed online, ask the helpdesk which official channel handles the specific field.

Mobile number, Aadhaar and bank safety

Registration and identity verification can involve sensitive personal information. Use a private device where possible, check the web address carefully and do not allow an unknown person to retain copies of identity documents. An authorised operator should not ask for a bank PIN, ATM PIN, CVV or UPI PIN. OTPs should be entered only in the official verification flow and never dictated to a caller who contacts you unexpectedly.

What workers should keep for future reference

  • The UAN and a copy of the e-Shram card, stored safely.
  • The mobile number used for registration and access to it for verification.
  • Any application acknowledgement or correction reference number.
  • Details of the occupation and address submitted in the record.
  • Official notices for any separate welfare scheme for which the worker applies.

Common misconceptions

An e-Shram card is not a universal benefit card, a guarantee of monthly money or proof that a worker has been approved for every government scheme. It is a database registration designed to support identification and social-security planning. Claims of a guaranteed payment in exchange for a registration fee should be treated as suspicious unless confirmed by a current government notification. The official portal and its published helpdesk are the safest places to verify a claim.

Advice for families and community service centres

Family members helping an older worker or someone unfamiliar with online forms should read each field aloud, confirm the information before submission and provide the acknowledgement after registration. Community service centres should display the official service charges, if any, and give a receipt. Workers should not hand over original documents for an extended period. A careful, accurate registration is more useful than a rushed application containing errors.
