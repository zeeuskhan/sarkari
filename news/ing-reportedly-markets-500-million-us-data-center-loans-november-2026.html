ING Reportedly Markets $500 Million Portfolio of U.S. Data-Center Loans

Published November 6, 2026 · 2:33 p.m. ET · Rajasthan News 24 US Desk

ING is reportedly offering a portfolio of about $500 million in loans tied to U.S. data centers, according to Reuters citing a Bloomberg News report and people familiar with the matter.

NEW YORK, November 6, 2026: ING is reportedly offering a portfolio of about $500 million in loans tied to U.S. data centers, according to Reuters citing a Bloomberg News report and people familiar with the matter.

What the reported portfolio includes

The reported portfolio contains 12 loans, with bids of at least $10 million due Nov. 19, according to the report. One loan includes about $25 million tied to a large New Mexico data center associated with the Stargate AI venture backed by OpenAI, SoftBank and Oracle. The portfolio also includes debt connected to operators Vantage Data Centers and CyrusOne.

What is confirmed and what is not

Reuters said ING declined to comment. The bank has not publicly confirmed the sale in the reporting available for this story, so the portfolio size, borrowers and bidding timetable should be understood as reported details, not a bank announcement.

A loan sale does not by itself mean a borrower has defaulted, that a project is distressed or that a lender expects losses. No reason for this reported sale has been confirmed.

Why data-center financing is drawing attention

AI data centers require large investments in land, buildings, power connections, cooling systems and computing equipment. Banks, private-credit funds and infrastructure investors are increasingly part of the financing chain. As spending rises, lenders and investors are paying closer attention to power availability, construction costs, customer commitments and the time required for projects to generate cash flow.

The reported sale is a development in the market for data-center debt, not proof of a broader credit problem. Any assessment of risk would require details on the individual loans, collateral, borrowers and terms that have not been made public in the report.
