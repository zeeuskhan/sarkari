Wendy’s Rejects $1.4 Billion Buyout Offer and Adopts One-Year Shareholder Rights Plan

Published November 6, 2026 · 2:04 p.m. ET · Rajasthan News 24 US Desk

Wendy’s independent directors unanimously rejected a $7.35-per-share cash proposal from an investor group led by Trian Partners, saying it undervalued the company. The board also adopted a one-year shareholder rights plan.

DUBLIN, OHIO, November 6, 2026: Wendy’s independent directors unanimously rejected a $7.35-per-share cash proposal from an investor group led by Trian Partners, saying it undervalued the company. The board also adopted a one-year shareholder rights plan.

Wendy’s rejected an unsolicited cash proposal from an investor group led by Trian Fund Management, which offered $7.35 per share for the shares it does not already own. The company’s independent committee said the proposal substantially undervalued Wendy’s and did not reflect the potential of its turnaround strategy, called Dave’s Way.

Why Wendy’s adopted a rights plan

The board adopted a limited-duration shareholder rights plan, often called a poison pill, on November 4. Under the plan, the rights can become exercisable if a person or group acquires 15% or more of Wendy’s common stock without board approval, or if an investor already above that threshold buys additional shares without approval.

The plan is scheduled to expire on November 3, 2027, unless the board ends it earlier. It is designed to give directors time to evaluate a takeover proposal and protect shareholders from an acquisition the board believes does not offer an appropriate premium. It does not prevent the board from considering an offer it determines is fair and in shareholders’ interests.

Who made the offer?

The investor group includes Trian, Flynn Group, BlueFive Capital and Sixth Street Partners. Wendy’s said the group already owned 16.3% of the company. The proposal was non-binding, so the rejection does not end the possibility of further discussions, a revised offer or a proxy contest.

The business challenge

Wendy’s is trying to revive customer traffic and restaurant profitability through menu, marketing, operations and digital changes. The company faces pressure from food costs, competition and older locations. The board’s decision puts the focus on whether management can deliver the turnaround value it says is greater than the current proposal.

A shareholder rights plan can deter an unsolicited accumulation of shares, but it does not guarantee a successful turnaround or determine the company’s future market value.
