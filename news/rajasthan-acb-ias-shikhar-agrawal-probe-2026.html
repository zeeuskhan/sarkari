Rajasthan ACB Probe Examines Property Transactions Linked to IAS Officer Shikhar Agrawal

Rajasthan's Anti-Corruption Bureau is examining alleged property transactions and benefits involving IAS officer Shikhar Agrawal. The allegations remain under investigation.

Published November 1, 2026 · Updated November 2, 2026 · Rajasthan News 24 Desk

Jaipur, November 1, 2026: Rajasthan's Anti-Corruption Bureau is examining alleged property transactions and possible benefits involving senior IAS officer Shikhar Agrawal. The inquiry concerns properties and resort-related dealings in Udaipur and Alwar, as well as questions around government concessions, according to reports by Indian Express, Times of India and NDTV.

Reports have cited different figures for the properties and their estimated value. The figures should therefore be treated as reported allegations, not as a final finding by investigators. The ACB inquiry is ongoing, and the existence of an investigation does not establish wrongdoing.

What is under examination?

The reported inquiry focuses on the acquisition and ownership of resort-linked properties, the financial transactions associated with them and whether any official decisions or concessions were improperly obtained. Investigators are expected to verify documents, ownership records and the sequence of relevant administrative decisions.

What have authorities clarified?

Media reports have noted that the ACB's stated inquiry at the time was focused on Agrawal. Separate administrative action involving IPS officer Prashakha Mathur was also reported. These developments should not be conflated: an administrative posting decision is not proof of criminal liability.

What happens next?

The ACB will need to establish the facts through records, statements and any other admissible evidence. Any conclusions about responsibility should be based on the outcome of the investigation and due legal process. Rajasthan News 24 will distinguish confirmed official findings from allegations as more information becomes available.

What an anti-corruption probe can involve

An investigation by the Anti-Corruption Bureau can involve collecting documentary records, examining financial transactions, recording statements and checking whether assets or transactions require explanation under the applicable law. The precise powers used depend on the stage of the case and the legal authorisation obtained. A preliminary inquiry, a formal investigation, a search, an arrest and the filing of a charge sheet are distinct steps and should not be described as if they are the same event.

Why property transactions may be examined

Property records can help investigators establish ownership, transaction dates, declared consideration, financing sources and relationships between parties. Investigators may compare sale deeds, registration records, bank transactions, income declarations and other material where legally relevant. A property purchase or sale by itself does not establish wrongdoing. The central question in a corruption case is whether evidence supports the specific allegation and whether the transaction can be explained through lawful sources and documentation.

What due process requires

People named in an inquiry are entitled to the protections provided by law, including the presumption of innocence and the right to respond through the legal process. Investigative agencies must follow applicable procedures for collecting evidence and taking coercive steps. A media report about an inquiry should not be treated as a finding of guilt. The outcome depends on the evidence, the investigating agency's conclusions and any subsequent judicial proceedings.

How readers should interpret official updates

  • Check whether the ACB has issued a formal statement or whether the information is attributed to unnamed sources.
  • Distinguish a preliminary inquiry from registration of an FIR or a charge sheet.
  • Do not treat an allegation, notice or questioning as a conviction.
  • Look for court records or official documents before accepting claims about amounts or assets.
  • Note whether the person concerned or their representative has issued a response.

What may happen next

Depending on the evidence and procedural stage, investigators may seek further records, examine additional transactions, question relevant persons or close an inquiry if allegations are not substantiated. If a formal case proceeds, the next steps may include a police report or charge sheet and hearings before the competent court. Timelines vary and should not be predicted without an official schedule.

Why careful reporting matters

Corruption allegations are matters of public interest, but accuracy and fairness are essential. Reporting should identify what the agency has confirmed, what remains an allegation and what has not been independently established.
