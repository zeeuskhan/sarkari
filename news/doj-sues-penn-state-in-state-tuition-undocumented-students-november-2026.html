Justice Department Sues Penn State Over In-State Tuition for Undocumented Students

Published November 6, 2026 · 2:04 p.m. ET · Rajasthan News 24 US Desk

The U.S. Department of Justice filed a lawsuit against Penn State, arguing that the university’s tuition policy violates federal law. The complaint seeks an injunction; the allegations have not been decided by a court.

WASHINGTON, November 6, 2026: The U.S. Department of Justice filed a lawsuit against Penn State, arguing that the university’s tuition policy violates federal law. The complaint seeks an injunction; the allegations have not been decided by a court.

The U.S. Department of Justice filed a lawsuit on November 5 challenging Pennsylvania State University’s policy of offering in-state tuition to some undocumented students who meet Pennsylvania residency criteria. The department alleges the policy gives a reduced tuition benefit to students who are not lawfully present in the United States while denying the same benefit to some U.S. citizens who do not meet the state’s residency rules.

What the federal complaint asks for

The Justice Department says the policy conflicts with federal law and asks a court to block Penn State from enforcing it. The complaint also seeks to prevent the university from adopting future tuition policies that, in the department’s view, favor undocumented students over U.S. citizens.

Those are allegations in a lawsuit, not a court ruling. A Penn State spokesperson said the university was reviewing the complaint, according to the Associated Press. No final judgment had been issued when this report was prepared.

Why the issue matters

In-state tuition can be substantially lower than out-of-state tuition, so eligibility rules affect college affordability. States and public universities have adopted different residency policies. Federal law has been the subject of repeated disputes over whether a state can offer certain higher-education benefits to undocumented residents unless the same benefits are available to U.S. citizens regardless of state residency.

The Penn State case is part of a broader federal campaign challenging state and university tuition policies. The Justice Department said the filing brought its total to 27 lawsuits on the issue. That is the department’s count and does not mean all cases have been resolved in its favor.

What happens next?

The case will proceed through federal court. Penn State can respond to the complaint, and a judge will decide whether the government has shown a legal basis for the requested relief. Students should not assume tuition rates have changed solely because a lawsuit was filed; any change would depend on court orders and university action.
