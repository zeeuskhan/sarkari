OpenAI Reaffirms U.S. Data-Center Expansion and Promises More Community Oversight

Published November 6, 2026 · 2:04 p.m. ET · Rajasthan News 24 US Desk

OpenAI said it will continue expanding U.S. data-center capacity despite political uncertainty after the midterm elections. The company also published voluntary commitments on power costs, water use, public reporting and local hiring.

SAN FRANCISCO, November 6, 2026: OpenAI said it will continue expanding U.S. data-center capacity despite political uncertainty after the midterm elections. The company also published voluntary commitments on power costs, water use, public reporting and local hiring.

OpenAI plans to continue expanding its U.S. data-center footprint, Chief Financial Officer Sarah Friar said in a CNBC interview reported by Reuters on Friday. The company is pointing to continued customer demand for AI computing even as investors and local communities debate the cost, scale and impact of new infrastructure.

What OpenAI promised communities

On November 5, OpenAI published a National Community Compact and a Compute Campus Policy Blueprint. The company said every future campus it owns and operates will have a local compact, with early engagement so residents can influence plans and a local point of contact throughout the project.

The commitments include paying the full cost of electricity and infrastructure used by a campus, providing financial assurances before major spending, developing a water plan, prioritizing lower-water cooling where possible, and publishing site-level water and electricity use. OpenAI also said it would publish construction plans and independent noise studies before operations begin.

The company said it will not use nondisclosure agreements with public officials except for narrow protections involving legitimate security information, trade secrets or competitively sensitive material. It also pledged to publish expected job creation and report on local hiring and apprenticeships.

Why data centers are a political issue

Large AI campuses can require substantial electricity, grid upgrades, water infrastructure, land and construction capacity. Communities have raised concerns about utility bills, noise, water use and whether local residents receive enough of the economic benefit.

What the commitments do not guarantee

The National Community Compact is a company commitment for future campuses OpenAI owns and operates. The broader blueprint proposes policies for lawmakers and regulators; it is not a federal or state law. Individual projects will still depend on local permits, utility agreements, financing and site-specific conditions.
