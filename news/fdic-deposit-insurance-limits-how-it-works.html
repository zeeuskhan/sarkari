FDIC Deposit Insurance: What Is Covered and What Is Not

FDIC Deposit Insurance: What Is Covered and What Is Not. A practical US guide explaining the rules, key decisions, steps to take and official resources for American consumers.

Published November 6, 2026 · Rajasthan News 24 US Desk · Educational guide

What Americans should know

The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation insures eligible deposits at FDIC-insured banks. Coverage is generally calculated per depositor, per insured bank, for each account ownership category. The standard maximum is $250,000 for each depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category, subject to the detailed rules.

How the rules and choices work

Checking accounts, savings accounts, money market deposit accounts and certificates of deposit are generally covered when held at an insured bank. Stocks, bonds, mutual funds, crypto assets, annuities and safe-deposit-box contents are not FDIC-insured deposits. A brokerage cash sweep or fintech arrangement requires checking the actual legal structure and participating institutions.

Practical steps to take

Use FDIC's Electronic Deposit Insurance Estimator or speak with the bank about account ownership categories and balances. Joint accounts, retirement accounts and trust deposits can have different calculation rules. Keep records of the bank's legal name and where each deposit is held, especially when a financial app uses partner banks.

Common mistakes and warning signs

The $250,000 limit is not simply per account or per branch. Multiple accounts in the same ownership category at one bank may be aggregated. Insurance also does not guarantee immediate access to every service during a bank resolution, so keep practical payment access in mind.

Where to verify current information

Rules, eligibility thresholds, fees and deadlines can change. Start with the official FDIC resource below, then check the state agency or plan administrator responsible for your specific situation. Keep dated copies of forms, notices, receipts and account messages. When a decision affects taxes, housing, healthcare, credit or legal rights, consider qualified professional or local legal assistance rather than relying on a general online article.

Frequently asked questions

Are these rules the same in every state?

Not always. Federal programmes have nationwide rules, but states can administer benefits differently and may add consumer protections, deadlines, licensing requirements or eligibility conditions. Check the official agency for the state where you live or work.

Can I rely on an online estimate?

Use calculators and general guides for planning, not as an official approval or final decision. Confirm important figures with the responsible agency, lender, insurer, plan administrator or tax professional using current information.

What should I keep for my records?

Save applications, account statements, contracts, notices, receipts, confirmation numbers and correspondence. A clear timeline and complete records make it easier to correct errors or ask for a review.
