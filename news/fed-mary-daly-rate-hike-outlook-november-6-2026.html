Fed’s Mary Daly Says More Rate Tightening May Be Needed as Inflation Remains Too High

Published November 6, 2026 · 2:04 p.m. ET · Rajasthan News 24 US Desk

SAN FRANCISCO, November 6, 2026: Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco President Mary Daly said she expects the central bank may need to tighten policy further to bring inflation down. She did not specify when or by how much, and her comments are not an FOMC decision.

Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco President Mary Daly said Friday that further monetary tightening is likely to be needed if inflation remains too high. In a CNBC interview, she said: “My expectation is, we’re going to have to tighten policy further.” She also emphasized that the timing and amount are not yet clear.

Daly’s view is not a Fed decision

Daly’s comments represent her own assessment as a regional Federal Reserve Bank president. The federal funds rate is set by the Federal Open Market Committee, which makes decisions collectively. A single official’s remarks do not change the target rate or guarantee that the committee will vote for an increase.

Daly said policymakers need to account for volatility, collect more information and proceed deliberately when the outlook is uncertain. She also described the recent repricing of technology shares as painful but orderly, linking market turmoil to questions about AI demand and investment.

Why the jobs report matters

The October employment report showed payrolls increasing by 165,000 and unemployment easing to 4.1%. Average hourly earnings reached $38.00, with annual wage growth reported at 3.1%. Stronger hiring can support household income, but it can also keep demand firm enough to complicate the Fed’s effort to bring inflation down.

Jobs data alone do not determine policy. Consumer prices, inflation expectations, credit conditions and the broader outlook all matter. If the Fed raises rates, some variable-rate debt may become more expensive, while mortgage and auto-loan rates will also depend on market yields and lender pricing.

The next scheduled FOMC meeting is December 8–9, 2026. Daly’s comments show that at least one policymaker is open to more tightening, but the committee’s decision will depend on the full set of economic data.
