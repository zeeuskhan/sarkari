US Midterm Elections 2026: Trump Campaigns in Iowa and Kansas as Election Day Nears

More than 52 million votes had reportedly been cast early as President Donald Trump campaigned in Iowa and Kansas ahead of the November 3 midterm elections.

Published November 1, 2026 · Rajasthan News 24 Desk · Updated as verified information develops

November 1, 2026: President Donald Trump is making campaign stops in Iowa and Kansas in the final days before the United States midterm elections on Tuesday, November 3. The Associated Press reported that more than 52 million votes had already been cast early as both parties focused on competitive contests that could shape control of Congress.

Trump's stops in Dubuque, Iowa, and Salina, Kansas, come as Republicans seek to retain control of the Senate and House of Representatives. The AP report highlighted Iowa's Senate contest and a Kansas race in which Democrat Adam Hamilton is seeking a Senate seat for his party.

Issues shaping the campaign

Voters' concerns about tariffs, the cost of living and the war involving Iran have become part of the political debate. Republican candidates are also trying to balance support for the president's agenda with local concerns in their states. Democrats are seeking to turn the midterms into a referendum on the administration, while facing their own challenges in closely contested races.

Why the midterms matter

All 435 seats in the House of Representatives and roughly one-third of the Senate seats are contested in a regular midterm cycle. The results will determine the balance of power in Congress and influence committee leadership, oversight and the passage of legislation for the remainder of the presidential term.

When will results be known?

Poll closing times differ by state, and vote counting may continue after election night. News organizations use official vote totals and established projection methods before declaring a race. A projection is not the same as a certified result. Readers should check state election offices for official counts and certification updates.

This article describes campaign developments reported on November 1. It does not predict which party or candidate will win.
